## 4. Top three directions in detail

### 4.1 C5 — How reliable are RAG design conclusions?

**Plain-language version.** Papers and engineering teams say things like "semantic chunking doesn't beat fixed-size
chunking" or "reranking helps". Each claim was measured with one dataset, one generator LLM, one judge, one prompt
and one random seed. If you change any of these reasonable choices, does the claim still hold? We would measure,
across all those reasonable choices together (the "multiverse"), how often common RAG design conclusions flip, which
choices cause the flips, and how many test questions you need before a conclusion can be trusted.

**Working title.** *How Stable Are RAG Design Conclusions? A Multiverse Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented Generation Evaluation*

**Research problem.** Let a *design claim* be "configuration A beats configuration B on metric M". Its truth is
evaluated under *nuisance choices* N (dataset, query sample, generator, judge, prompt, seed, top-k). We ask how the
sign and significance of Δ = M(A) − M(B) vary over the space of defensible N, and which components of N drive that
variation.

**Why it matters.** Teams make costly design decisions from these comparisons, and papers report them as general
findings. If conclusions are configuration-specific, both practice and science are being misled. A well-done
negative result ("the conclusions are robust") is also valuable.

**State of the art (L1).**
- Statistics for evaluation: Miller 2024 (error bars, power for LM evals); Heineman et al. 2025 (signal/noise of
  benchmarks); IR significance testing (Smucker et al., CIKM 2007; Urbano et al. 2019).
- Multiverse analysis in ML: Bell et al., NeurIPS 2022.
- RAG design sensitivity: Zhao et al. (TOSEM 2026; optimal retrieval volume is task-dependent); BERGEN
  (Findings of EMNLP 2024; standardised RAG benchmarking, 500+ experiments).
- A precedent: Nie et al. 2026 show poisoning-attack conclusions fail once realistic chunking and reranking are added.

**Evidence for the gap (hypothesis).** My searches (#17, #28) found **no multiverse or conclusion-stability analysis
of RAG design choices**. General eval-statistics work exists but is not applied to RAG pipeline decisions, and IR
significance testing covers retrieval only, not generation or judges.

**Strongest threat.** BERGEN or the TOSEM design-decisions paper may already report ranking stability. **Both must be
read in full before we commit (Phase 1, task L-1).**

**Potential contribution.**
1. The first quantitative estimate of how often common RAG design claims flip across defensible evaluation choices.
2. A variance decomposition showing which choices matter (for example dataset × design interaction vs judge vs seed).
3. Sample-size and power guidance for RAG design comparisons.
4. An open-source multiverse harness, so others can stress-test their own claims.

**Research questions.**
- RQ1: For commonly studied RAG design choices (chunking strategy and size, retriever type, reranking, top-k), in what
  fraction of defensible evaluation configurations is the direction of the effect reversed, and in what fraction is
  it statistically significant?
- RQ2: What share of the variance in Δ is attributable to dataset, query sample, generator, judge, prompt and seed?
- RQ3: How many paired queries are needed to detect typical RAG design effects at 80% power, and what share of
  published comparisons fall below that?

**Falsifiable hypotheses** (thresholds to be fixed in pre-registration *before* any data is seen).
- H1: For at least one widely studied choice, Δ changes sign in ≥ 20% of configurations.
  *Falsified if* every studied choice has a sign-flip rate below 20%.
- H2: Dataset × design interaction explains more variance in Δ than generator sampling noise.
  *Falsified if* the seed/sampling component is larger.
- H3: For LLM-judged metrics, judge identity explains ≥ 10% of variance in Δ, while for retrieval metrics (no judge)
  the corresponding share is near zero. The second half is a built-in sanity check.
- H4: Detecting a 2-point paired difference at 80% power needs more queries than the median test-set size used in
  the comparisons we survey.

**Methodology.** Factorial or sampled multiverse. Paired bootstrap or permutation tests within each configuration,
with multiple-comparison control. Mixed-effects models with query random effects for the variance decomposition.
Specification-curve plots. Run the retrieval stage first (deterministic and cheap), then generation.

**Candidate datasets** (to be verified for access and licence in Phase 1; not yet checked). Public QA/retrieval
collections with long source documents, so chunking actually matters; OHRBench raw documents (in bibliography);
financial-filing QA.

**Baselines.** The design alternatives themselves (fixed-size at 2-3 sizes, recursive, semantic, structure-aware;
BM25, dense, hybrid; reranker on/off), each under the same nuisance grid.

**Metrics.** Retrieval: Recall@k, nDCG@10, MRR. Answers: EM/F1 where gold answers exist, plus LLM-judged correctness
and faithfulness. Meta-metrics: sign-flip rate, significant-agreement rate, variance shares, required n.

**Expected challenges.** Defining "defensible" choices without biasing toward flips; the combinatorial cost of
generation; the dependence of judge-metric validity on the judge itself.

**Risks and limitations.** Novelty risk from BERGEN or the TOSEM paper. Reviewers may say "it's an analysis paper";
the answer is to release the harness and give actionable guidance. Results from small open models may not transfer
to frontier models, so include at least one strong model if budget allows.

**Minimum viable experiment (CPU-only, about 1-2 weeks of part-time work).** Retrieval-only multiverse on 2 datasets:
BM25 + 1 small dense embedder × 3 chunk sizes × 3 top-k × 50 bootstrap query resamples. Report the sign-flip rate of
"chunk size 256 vs 512" and "BM25 vs dense". No LLM needed. **Blocked** until huggingface.co (models/datasets) or
another dataset host is allowed.

**Venues (to verify per call).** SIGIR/ECIR/CIKM (including reproducibility tracks where offered), ACL/EMNLP
Findings, NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks (if the harness is a major contribution), evaluation workshops.

**PhD relevance.** Puts you in IR and NLP evaluation methodology, a strong, durable, cross-cutting skill. It shows you
can test a claim rather than just build a system.

### 4.2 C3 — Silent ingestion-layer faults in RAG

**Plain-language version.** RAG benchmarks assume the search index faithfully contains the documents. In production,
documents silently go missing (a parser fails, a file key is truncated), tables get cut off, deleted documents
linger, and metadata is lost. We would inject these faults deliberately and in controlled amounts into public corpora,
measure how much damage each does, and test whether the usual RAG quality checks notice. We would also test whether
cheap ingestion-time checks (counting and reconciling documents, canary documents, content hashes) catch what answer
checks miss.

**Working title.** *Silent Corruption Upstream: Measuring and Detecting Ingestion-Layer Faults in Retrieval-Augmented Generation*

**Problem.** For an ingestion fault f at corruption rate r, estimate the dose-response of retrieval and answer
quality, and the detection power of answer-level, retrieval-level and ingestion-level monitors.

**State of the art (L1).** The silent-failure fault-injection study (Elsevier journal, 2026) injects 12 faults at the
retrieval, context, prompt and fallback layers, including stale index, chunk-boundary corruption and duplicate
inflation, and shows answer-level checks miss evidence and citation damage. OHRBench (ICCV 2025) covers OCR noise.
"Seven Failure Points" (CAIN 2024) names *missing content* qualitatively. RARE (2026) covers redundancy. Practitioner
tools for index drift exist; no peer-reviewed controlled study of them was found.

**Evidence for the gap (hypothesis).** No study found that injects *ingestion-origin* faults (silent document loss
from parsing or key handling, partial structured-content extraction, failed deletion propagation, metadata and
provenance loss) and compares them with *ingestion-time* detectors. **The 2026 silent-failure study is the main
threat and must be read in full.**

**Potential contribution.** A practice-grounded fault taxonomy, an open fault injector, dose-response curves, and a
detectability comparison across monitoring layers.

**Research questions.**
- RQ1: Which ingestion faults degrade retrieval and answer quality most per unit of corruption?
- RQ2: Which faults are *silent*, meaning undetected by standard RAG metrics at a fixed evaluation budget?
- RQ3: Do source-to-index reconciliation, canary documents and hashing detect the silent faults, and at what
  false-positive rate?

**Hypotheses.**
- H1: Document-loss faults degrade quality roughly in proportion to the share of gold-evidence documents affected,
  while partial-structure faults degrade structure-dependent questions disproportionately.
- H2: At least one fault class lowers answer correctness beyond noise while LLM-judged faithfulness stays within noise.
- H3: Reconciliation detects document loss and staleness (AUROC ≥ 0.9) but not content-level corruption.

**Methods, data and metrics.** Controlled injection at rates {0, 1, 5, 10, 25}% on public raw-document corpora (OHRBench
candidates; financial filings; a synthetic structured-file corpus from open-source configuration files). BM25 and dense
pipelines. Metrics: Recall@k, answer correctness, judge faithfulness, detector AUROC and detection delay.

**Challenges and risks.** No public data on real fault prevalence, so the paper can claim sensitivity but not
frequency. Overlap with the 2026 study. Realistic simulation of parser failures.

**Minimum viable experiment (CPU-only).** Three faults (document drop, table truncation, stale duplicate) × 5 rates on
one corpus with BM25. Retrieval dose-response plus a reconciliation detector. No LLM needed.

**Venues.** CAIN, data-management-for-ML workshops, SIGIR/CIKM short or resource tracks, EMNLP Findings.

**PhD relevance.** Data-centric AI and ML-systems reliability, drawing directly on your strongest engineering experience.

### 4.3 C2 — Failure-signal contracts in tool-using agents

**Plain-language version.** When a tool fails, the agent harness decides how to tell the model: an empty "success",
an error code, or a structured diagnosis with hints. Your web-research agent showed that an empty success can look
like a real answer. Does the *format* of the failure signal change how often agents fabricate?

**Working title.** *What the Tool Says When It Fails: Failure-Signal Contracts and Fabrication in LLM Agents*

**State of the art (L1).** Tools Fail (EMNLP 2024): LLMs often accept silently wrong tool outputs, and prompting helps.
ToolFailBench (ICML 2026): fabrication and result-ignoring under contradicting tool returns. When Tools Fail,
AgentNoiseBench, AgentProp-Bench and SHIELDA (2025-26).

**Gap (weak hypothesis).** The signal format, holding the underlying failure fixed, as the manipulated variable. It
may already be covered inside AgentNoiseBench or When Tools Fail; neither has been read.

**RQs and hypotheses.** H1: empty-success signals produce more fabrication than explicit errors. H2: structured
diagnostics reduce fabrication without lowering task success. H3: effects differ by model family.

**MVE.** 3 signal formats × 2 open models × 200 synthetic tasks with mock tools. Needs a model runtime or API.

**Risks.** The highest scoop risk of the three; the likely ceiling is a workshop paper unless a sharper mechanism emerges.

**Why it is still third.** Cheap, directly from your experience, and it keeps an agent direction in play.
